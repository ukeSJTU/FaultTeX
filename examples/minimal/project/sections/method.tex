\section{Method}

Let $c$ denote a scientific claim and let $E = \{e_1, \ldots, e_n\}$ be the evidence
sentences returned by a retriever. An encoder maps each claim--evidence pair to a vector
$h_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$. CEA assigns separate relevance and confidence scores:

\begin{align}
  r_i &= \sigma(w_r^\top h_i), \\
  q_i &= \sigma(w_q^\top h_i), \\
  \alpha_i &= \frac{\exp(r_i q_i / \tau)}
  {\sum_{j=1}^{n} \exp(r_j q_j / \tau)},
\end{align}

where $\sigma$ is the logistic function and $\tau$ is a learned temperature. The final
representation $z = \sum_i \alpha_i h_i$ is passed to a linear classifier that predicts
\textsc{support}, \textsc{refute}, or \textsc{insufficient evidence}.

We optimize cross-entropy for the verification label together with a calibration loss
on held-out confidence targets. The auxiliary loss encourages the model to reduce the
weight assigned to evidence that is relevant in topic but unreliable for the specific
claim.
