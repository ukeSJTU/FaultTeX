\section{Introduction}

Automated claim verification has become an important component of fact-checking and
scientific document analysis. Early systems retrieve textual evidence and predict
whether it supports or refutes a claim~\cite{thorne2018fever}. Scientific verification
adds further difficulty because evidence is distributed across abstracts, tables, and
specialized terminology~\cite{wadden2020fact}.

Most neural verifiers treat every retrieved sentence as equally trustworthy. This
assumption is fragile when retrieval introduces topically related but irrelevant
sentences, or when evidence comes from sources with different levels of reliability.
We study whether explicit relevance and confidence estimates can make aggregation more
robust without requiring a substantially larger language model.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we introduce a calibrated aggregation module
that separates sentence relevance from source confidence. Second, we evaluate the
method under both standard and disagreement-heavy settings. Third, we provide ablations
that isolate the contribution of each scoring component.
